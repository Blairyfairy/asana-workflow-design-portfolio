# Workflow Socialization and Change Plan

## Objective

Help the Phoenix Airlines pilot cohort understand why the workflow is changing, how to use it, where to get support, and how feedback will be incorporated before broad rollout.

## Change rationale (“why statement”)

We are standardizing project intake and delivery so requests arrive with the information needed to make decisions, owners can see the next action, approvals are traceable, and leaders can understand demand and bottlenecks. The workflow should reduce avoidable follow-up and improve transparency; the pilot will test whether it actually does so.

## Pilot member selection

The sample cohort should represent the people who submit, triage, approve, and deliver work. Use role-based placeholders in `pilot_members.csv`, then replace them with real participants after manager approval.

Selection criteria:
- At least one frequent requester.
- At least one request coordinator or intake owner.
- At least one functional approver.
- At least one project delivery lead.
- At least one representative from a team affected by the workflow.
- Include different levels of Asana familiarity and accessibility needs.
- Confirm availability for both weeks of training and feedback.

## Communication plan

Use `communication_plan.csv` to plan:
- Sponsor announcement and reason for change.
- Pilot invitation and expectations.
- Launch-day quick-start guide.
- Mid-pilot feedback reminder.
- Pilot results and next-step decision.

Messages should explain what changes, what remains the same, where to submit requests, how urgent work is handled, and how users can get help.

## Two-week training plan

Use `two_week_training_plan.csv` as a starting schedule. It includes:
- Week 1: orientation, guided request submission, triage and approvals, task ownership, and office hours.
- Week 2: realistic practice, exception handling, reporting, feedback, and readiness review.

Training should be role-specific. Requesters need to know how to submit complete requests and follow status; coordinators need to know triage and routing; approvers need to record decisions; delivery owners need to maintain task health and dependencies.

## Organizational conventions

See `templates/conventions.md`. Conventions should cover naming, descriptions, owners, due dates, sections, custom fields, statuses, approvals, comments, attachments, and archival. Publish one approved source of truth and assign an owner to maintain it.

## Adoption measures

- Pilot invitation acceptance and training attendance.
- Percentage of pilot requests submitted through the new form.
- Intake completeness rate.
- Number of clarification loops per request.
- Time from submission to triage.
- User confidence rating before and after training.
- Feedback themes and resolution rate.
- Number of critical workflow issues still open at go/no-go.

Do not claim adoption success until actual pilot data has been collected and reviewed.
